# CSL Business Development and Pursuits

A pursuit is an owner's-representative deliverable about CSL itself, held to the same standard as a client report: every claim traceable to the portfolio in `csl-projects`, every requirement of the solicitation answered in the place the evaluator expects it, every date on a calendar, and a named CSL person deciding go or no-go. This skill covers the path from a solicitation notice or a client's request to a signed submittal or a level-of-effort proposal. Marketing (N. Graboyes) owns layout, graphics, references, and photos per `csl-brand`; the responsible Project Executive or Director owns the decision; Partners (M. Cammarota, E. LeFlore) own strategic pursuits and sign cover letters. Numbering per `csl-document-control` (`CSL-<PROG>-SOQ-<solicitation no.>`, `CSL-<PROG>-LOE-<yymmdd>`); the cost side of an LOE per `csl-cost-change-control` 3.10; staff titles per `csl-people`.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Scan and screen

Screen every notice against the criteria below in one pass; a notice that fails an early row is closed with one line in the pursuit log so nobody rescreens it next week.

| Criterion | Pass condition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Institution or agency sector | Higher education, healthcare, life sciences, advanced manufacturing, K-12, corporate, government, hospitality, non-profit | The portfolio in `csl-projects` supports these; evaluators score comparable experience first |
| Geography | Northeast core; southeast growth market anchored by the South Carolina projects (Cytiva, ErlingKlinger, North Charleston); east of the Mississippi for higher-education scans; Pensacola and the Gulf Coast for local public work | Staffing and travel cost decide fee competitiveness |
| Runway | At least one month between the screen and the submittal due date | Less than that produces a thin response; the 10-business-day rule already escalates it |
| Fit | Ideal fit, not adjacent: the solicitation asks for the role CSL performs | Adjacent fits cost the same to write and lose |
| Scope type | Capex or opex scope short of physical work: owner's representative, OPM, program or project management, program controls, tenant representation, equipment planning, mitigation and communications, relocation, dashboards and reporting | CSL does not hold trade contracts or perform construction |
| Gates | Every prequalification or registration gate identified with its lead time inside the runway | A gate discovered late ends the pursuit after the work is done |
| Mandatory events | Pre-submittal or pre-bid attendance requirements identified with date, time, zone, and location | Missing a mandatory event disqualifies the submittal regardless of content |

Gates seen on CSL pursuits: state OPM qualification requirements where the statute sets them (Massachusetts M.G.L. c.149 §44A½ above $1.5M); agency contractor prequalification (CFX); NATO Commercial Partner (NCP) application plus Declaration of Eligibility (DOE); SAM.gov registration and the solicitation's set-aside status for federal work; municipal vendor registration and state business registration for local public work.

### 3.3 Go/no-go memo

Written for the decision owner before any drafting hour is spent. Template: `templates/go-no-go-memo.md`; populated example in Section 6.

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Fit score table | Ten criteria, each scored 1 to 5 with the evidence in the row: sector; scope; geography; size; incumbency and competition; prequal and registration gates; deadline runway; team availability; price and fee posture; strategic value. A criterion with no evidence is "not established (score withheld)", never guessed |
| Reading the score (package convention) | Any gate that cannot clear inside the runway is a no-go on its own. Otherwise: 35 or more of 50 on scored criteria, go; 25 to 34, conditional go with the conditions named; below 25, no-go. Higher-education pursuits use the weighted matrix in the installed `higher-ed-bidding` skill instead |
| Recommendation | Go / conditional go / no-go, one sentence, then the conditions with owners and dates |
| Resourcing ask | Capture lead, writers, reviewers, Marketing production window, proposed team members to confirm, hours by role |
| Key dates | Pre-submittal (mandatory or not), questions deadline, addenda expected, internal draft and red-team dates, signature, delivery with time and zone |
| Decision owner | Responsible PX or Director for the go; Marketing Manager for production; Partners for strategic pursuits (new market, new sector, or fee above the PX threshold) |

### 3.4 Compliance matrix

Build it before the outline, because it is the outline. Every "shall", "must", "will provide", and "required" in the solicitation becomes a row. Template with the column definitions: `templates/compliance-matrix.md`.

1. Extract requirements in document order; one requirement per row; quote the operative words.
2. Columns: section reference | requirement | our response location (tab and heading) | owner | status (open / drafted / reviewed / final) | notes (addendum changes, page budget).
3. Separate lists beneath the matrix: forms and certifications with signatory; page limits, font and margin rules, tab order; submission logistics (portal or physical, copies, USB, time, zone, address).
4. Re-run the extraction after every addendum; mark changed rows with the addendum number.
5. The matrix is the red-team checklist: a reviewer reads the response against the matrix, not against the prose.

### 3.9 LOE proposal to an existing client

**Trigger.** A client asks for a proposal for defined work inside a running engagement. W77 case: the client program lead asked for a proposal to merge a building condition assessment sheet from about 2024 with the IRO roadmap into one maintained source with a PMO / IMT update cadence; CSL built a 348-record consolidated tracker as proof of concept on Thu 09/17/26 (`CSL-W77-TRK_Roadmap-IRO-Consolidated_2026-09-17.xlsx`); the LOE proposal was owed the week of 09/21/26.

**Structure** (`templates/loe-proposal.md`; number `CSL-<PROG>-LOE-<yymmdd>`): objective | current state, from the record with dates | proposed deliverable | method | cadence | roles and hours by role, by task | assumptions | exclusions | dependencies on client access | schedule | fee basis (rates by role per the CSL rate table referenced by contract and edition; no rates or totals typed into the narrative) | acceptance block. The cost side (hours basis, rate reference, fee summary, change triggers) is produced per `csl-cost-change-control` 3.10 and reviewed by the Project Director before issue. Keep the proposal to three pages; the proof of concept is the exhibit.

| Section | W77 content (from the record) |
|---|---|
| Objective | One maintained source combining the building condition assessment records (about 2024) with the IRO roadmap, updated on the PMO / IMT cadence, so the two are never reconciled by hand again |
| Current state | Two sources with different keys and vintages; the roadmap master live in Smartsheet (1059284113641348) pending migration to SharePoint; the assessment sheet static; proof of concept of 348 consolidated records built 09/17/26 |
| Proposed deliverable | The consolidated tracker as the maintained source (Smartsheet now, SharePoint list after migration), a data dictionary, a reconciliation log, and a monthly PMO / IMT update package |
| Method | Key mapping from the proof of concept; one-time reconciliation with a change list proposed to the client (never written to the client's sheet without instruction); then the maintenance cycle |
| Cadence | Weekly refresh with the Friday editions; monthly PMO / IMT package; quarterly re-baseline check |
| Roles | Project Manager (method, client review), Assistant PM (weekly refresh, reconciliation log), dashboard / data specialist (dictionary, views), Project Director (review) |
| Assumptions | Client supplies both sources and edit access; one review cycle per package; no new data sources in the base scope |
| Exclusions | Field verification of assessment records; new assessments; software licensing; dashboard rebuilds beyond the existing views |
| Dependencies | Access to the assessment sheet's current owner; SharePoint migration timing; PMO / IMT calendar |
| Fee basis | Hours by role by task against the CSL rate table for the engagement, referenced by contract and edition; totals in the cost side only |

### 3.9.1 What makes an LOE issuable

The request is cited by email date or minutes item; the current state carries dates and sheet IDs; the deliverable is named as a thing the client can point to; cadence and roles produce the hours, not the reverse; exclusions are as specific as the scope; and the acceptance block names the client signatory and the CSL signatory.

### 3.10 Capability statement and positioning piece

**Capability statement** (one page; `templates/capability-statement.md`): firm line; three differentiators; core services; sectors with named projects from `csl-projects`; certifications and registrations as Marketing holds them; contacts. Federal versions add SAM.gov UEI and CAGE, NAICS codes, and set-aside status as Marketing and the CFO hold them; the agent never types identifiers from memory.

**Positioning piece** (two pages; no solicitation exists): the program as publicly described, with sources; why a program of that scale needs an owner's project manager or owner's consultant (governance, controls, reporting, permits, turnover at scale); CSL's relevant proof, chosen by 3.6 (for Terafab: cleanroom and high-utility manufacturing, semiconductor-site infrastructure practice, program controls and dashboards); proposed roles CSL could fill; the next step (a named conversation, a capability briefing). Program facts are dated and sourced; nothing is asserted about the owner's intentions beyond what is published.

### 3.11 Pre-submittal meeting attendance

Attend every mandatory meeting in person unless the solicitation allows remote attendance; sign in under the firm's legal name. Capture: attendee list (firms present, roles, likely incumbents); every question and answer; addenda promised and their expected dates; evaluation criteria emphasized by the owner's staff; submission logistics restated (copies, portal, time, zone, address); site conditions or program facts stated for the first time.

Same day, an internal memo (`CSL-<PROG>-MEM-<yymmdd>`, one page) to the responsible PX/Director and Marketing, in this order:

1. Attendance confirmed: date, time, zone, location, sign-in evidence filed.
2. Program facts stated by the owner that change the response (scope, budget range, schedule, delivery method, incumbents).
3. Questions and answers, numbered; which answers will appear in an addendum and when.
4. Evaluation emphasis heard from the owner's staff, in their words.
5. Firms present, with the likely role of each (prime, sub, incumbent).
6. Changes to the compliance matrix (rows added, changed, removed) and to the go/no-go (score changes, new conditions).
7. Next actions with owners and dates.

The memo is filed with the pursuit and cited in the SOQ's addenda acknowledgment when the Q&A is later issued as an addendum.

### 3.12 Production and version control

- Marketing owns layout, graphics, cover, and tab design per `csl-brand`; the agent drafts content in Word with `skills/csl-brand/assets/docx_styles.py` (`new_document`, `add_heading`, `add_table`, `add_document_control_block`) so styles carry into Marketing's template without rework.
- The compliance matrix is the drafting outline; every section heading maps to matrix rows, and the matrix row cites the heading.
- Document number `CSL-<PROG>-SOQ-<solicitation no.>` (`CSL-PNS-SOQ-26-062`); drafts carry the number with a DRAFT watermark; revisions `R1`, `R2` per `csl-document-control`; file names `CSL-PNS-SOQ-26-062_2026-09-22_R2.docx`.
- One working file per volume (technical, fee, forms); the fee volume is never merged into the technical file.
- Red-team review against the matrix by someone who did not draft, at least five business days before the due date; Partner or VP signature two business days before; delivery one day early for physical submittals.

### 3.13 Pursuit log and weekly cadence

Keep one pursuit log for the firm (Smartsheet or the Marketing tracker), one row per notice screened, so the same solicitation is never rescreened and the 10-business-day rule can be checked in one view.

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Notice | Agency or institution; solicitation number; title; program code once assigned |
| Source and date seen | Portal or email; date the notice was first read |
| Screen result | Pass / closed, with the failing row from 3.1 when closed |
| Gates | Each gate with lead time and status |
| Key dates | Pre-submittal (mandatory or not); questions; due date, time, zone |

| Status | Screening / go-no-go / drafting / red team / submitted / shortlisted / awarded / lost / closed |
| Outcome and debrief | Result; owner's debrief notes; what to change next time |

Weekly, on the day Marketing runs its scan: new notices screened and logged; every open pursuit's days to due date recomputed; any pursuit inside 10 business days without a go/no-go escalated the same day; addenda checked on every open portal; the log's status column read out to the responsible PX/Director in one paragraph.

## 5. Voice and formatting rules

- First person plural; owner's-advocate stance; no marketing adjectives ("world-class", "unparalleled"). The proof line does the work.
- Every project citation in the exact `csl-projects` form; every metric with its source row; unknowns flagged to Marketing, never estimated.
- Client-executive prose: no dash constructions; acronyms spelled out on first use (Owner's Project Manager, Statement of Qualifications).
- Dates MM/DD/YY with day of week for deadlines; times with zone (CT for Pensacola pursuits, ET for the northeast).
- Requirements answered in the evaluator's order and vocabulary; headings mirror the solicitation's words.
- Fee content lives only in the fee volume; the technical volume never states a rate, total, or multiplier.
- A staff member's outside ventures or non-CSL work are never referenced in CSL pursuit material.

## 7. Failure modes and how to avoid them

| Failure | What it looks like | Avoidance |
|---|---|---|
| Chasing adjacent fits | A CM-Build or an ITB for physical work pursued as if it were an owner's rep role | Screen row "Fit: ideal, not adjacent"; DASNY 7637 and CFX 270702 as the precedents |
| Discovering a prequal gate late | Prequalification, NCP, or registration lead time longer than the remaining runway | Gates identified with lead times in the screen, before any drafting hour |
| Missing a mandatory pre-bid | The submittal is rejected unread | Mandatory events in the go/no-go key dates with a named attendee and sign-in evidence |
| Citing projects with invented metrics | A square footage or contract value not in `csl-projects` | Exact names and recorded attributes only; Marketing flag for anything else |
| Approach text with no proof | Paragraphs that could describe any firm | Every block carries a proof line from Section 3.7 |
| Fee in the wrong envelope | A rate table inside the technical volume | Separate volume; matrix row for the fee instruction; red team checks it |
| Page-limit violations | Appendices counted by the owner and not by the writer | Page budget per section in the matrix; count as the RFQ counts |
| No named decision owner | Drafting starts on a hallway yes | Go/no-go memo with the decision owner initialed before hour one |
| Deadline inside 10 business days, no decision | The pursuit is discovered in week two | Same-day escalation per the matrix with the memo attached |
| Verbal client request treated as scope | Work on an LOE started before the request is in writing | Request email or minutes item cited in the LOE's current-state section |
| Cross-branding | Outside-venture material or logos in a CSL pursuit | Never; CSL material only |

## 8. Pre-submission checklist

- Go/no-go memo on file with the decision owner's initials; conditions closed or carried with owners.
- Compliance matrix complete; every row's response location verified in the final file; addenda acknowledged by number.
- Mandatory events attended with sign-in evidence filed.
- Gates cleared and evidenced (registration numbers, prequalification letters, NCP / DOE, SAM.gov active).
- Projects cited by exact name with recorded attributes only; references, dates, and photos supplied by Marketing.
- Team confirmed by the PX; resumes in Marketing's template; availability statement by phase; org chart on one page.
- Approach blocks each carry a proof line; no marketing adjectives; no dash constructions in executive prose.
- Fee volume separate and sealed per the solicitation; technical volume free of rates and totals.
- Page limits, font, margins, and tab order verified against the solicitation; page count taken the owner's way.
- Forms signed by the named signatory; cover letter signed by a Partner or VP.
- Document number, revision, file name per `csl-document-control`; DRAFT watermark removed on the issued copy.
- Brand QA per `csl-brand` run on the assembled PDF; logo file official, not the CSS fallback.
- Delivery logistics confirmed: method, copies, USB, address or portal, time and zone; one day early for physical delivery.
- Pursuit folder holds the solicitation, addenda, matrix, memos, drafts, and the submitted file with its receipt.
