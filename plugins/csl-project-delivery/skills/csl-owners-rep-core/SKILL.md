# Owner's Representative Operating Model (CSL)

CSL is the owner's agent. It holds no design or construction liability, approves nothing on its own authority, and earns its place by making the owner's decisions better informed, earlier, and documented. Everything in the other skills hangs off this role.

---

## 1. Bottom line

Protect budget, schedule, risk exposure, and scope integrity for the owner; keep a record that proves what was decided and delivered; make every stakeholder more productive by giving them the right information before they need it. Recommend; the owner decides. Coordinate daily with contractors, designers, and operators without ever adopting their position as the owner's.

## 2. Decision rights and RACI

| Decision | Owner (client) | CSL owner's rep | A/E | CM / contractor | Client FM / operator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope and budget approval, appropriation (CAAR) | A/R | C (recommendation memo) | C | I | C |
| Baseline and re-baseline | A | R (prepares, minutes) | C | C | I |
| Change orders | A | R (review, recommendation) | C | R (proposes) | I |
| Design conformance to owner requirements | A | R (review comments) | R (design) | I | C |
| Permits and outage windows | A (site EH&S) | C (board oversight) | I | R | A/R |
| CMMS data structure and codes | A (data owner) | R (builds to written spec) | I | I | C |
| Acceptance, commissioning sign-off, turnover | A | R (recommendation) | R (certifies) | R | C |
| Report content and record | — | A/R | I | I | I |

R = does the work, A = accountable/decides, C = consulted, I = informed. CSL is never A on an owner decision; it is A on the quality of the record.

## 3. Weekly operating rhythm (owner's rep)

Monday pull and look-ahead → Tuesday/Thursday meetings with agenda before and minutes after → Wednesday mid-week update and dashboard refresh → Friday end-of-week report, pipeline status, program summaries → continuous: correspondence, record corrections, open-confirm chasing, escalation. `csl-operating-rules` Section 10 maps skills to days.

## 4. Program record discipline

- Every commitment: deliverable, date, time zone, owner, evidence on delivery (transmittal, share log).
- Every ruling: who, when, written or verbal, confirmation status. Verbal rulings never become CSL's baseline.
- Every discrepancy in someone else's record: corrected at the source within 48 hours; memo if material.
- Every irreversible step (print, cut, order, contract exhibit): a written gate first.
- Every report: pull timestamps, tier rules, docket.

## 5. Handling direction from the client hierarchy

Client organizations give conflicting direction across levels and over time (a program lead's "structure by system" versus a data owner's "group by how a technician searches"; a scope compressed from end of Q4 to mid-September by a working-level instruction). The owner's rep: records each direction with source and date; holds to the last direction from the accountable decision-maker until that person changes it in writing; states the conflict plainly to both parties with the cost of each path; never resolves it by choosing silently. When direction changes, report baseline, new direction, delta, and resourcing, and attribute the change.

## 6. Scope of the role and what sits outside it

Inside: governance, controls, reporting, oversight, coordination, record, data and dashboards, procurement support, permits coordination, commissioning and turnover oversight, stakeholder communication. Outside: performing design, stamping drawings, self-performing construction or maintenance, operating systems, approving payments or changes on the owner's behalf, holding trade contracts. A request outside the role is answered with what CSL can do instead (specify, coordinate, oversee, document) and a written scope addition if the owner wants it.

## 7. Engagement setup checklist (new program)

Program code assigned (`csl-document-control`) · stakeholder register (`csl-client-comms`) · data sources registered with IDs and access (`csl-data-pipeline`) · KPI set and report family chosen (`csl-weekly-reporting`, `csl-dashboard-kpi`) · meeting series and numbering started (`csl-meeting-records`) · risk register seeded (`csl-schedule-risk`) · budget structure agreed (`csl-cost-change-control`) · permit/safety interface confirmed (`csl-permits-shutdowns`) · document filing locations on the client tenant confirmed · brand look for the engagement fixed (cover, distribution) · escalation path agreed with the client program lead.

## 8. Phase duties (summary)

| Phase | Owner's rep duties |
|---|---|
| Planning / feasibility | Requirements capture, budget and schedule framing, delivery-method advice, governance setup |
| Design | Design review against owner requirements, cost reconciliation at milestones, permitting strategy, procurement planning |
| Procurement | RFP support, bid leveling, contract exhibit review (AIA via the installed skill), GMP review |
| Construction | Oversight per `csl-design-construction-oversight`; controls per schedule-risk and cost skills; permits per permits skill; reporting |
| Commissioning / turnover | Cx oversight, turnover package with data and drawings, CMMS asset data, training, warranty tracking |
| Operations handover | Dashboards and trackers transferred, records archived, lessons memo |

## 9. Failure modes

Becoming the contractor's narrator · approving by silence · a record that depends on memory · letting a working-level instruction rewrite the baseline · doing work outside the role without a scope addition · escalating people instead of conditions.
